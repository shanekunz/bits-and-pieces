### **Practical Truths After the Hype**
#### Now Let’s Get Back to Building

No matter how disruptive or life changing the LLM “revolution” has been for you or anyone else, it helps to view the whole situation from a future historical context, not emotionally, not like a bull or bear counting on an ROI.

## Neural Nets and LLMs Are Smart Switch Statements
On one hand this point is so obvious it feels trite, but on the other hand nobody seems to remind us in papers or hot takes. No programming language has been replaced by an LLM. It is a tool called from regular code. At that moment it becomes exactly what it is, a smart switch statement.

```javascript
switch(randomData) {
	case "A": optionA();
	case "B": optionB();
	// etc
}
```

Increase the size of `randomData`, crank up the number of branches, run this hundreds of times per second... it is still choosing an option. That does not downplay its value, making cheap, wide-open smart switch statements available to everyone is fantastic, but starting here keeps our feet on the ground.

## Neural Nets Don’t Output Art
Human art is communication. It is imperfect on purpose. The Mona Lisa matters because it sparks conversation, not because it is the “most perfect” painting ever made. People keep warning me AI will take my job, but will it spin up every app on the App Store with its own self-directed willpower, deliberate feature trade-offs, and a product roadmap? Does taste require first being a taster?

## Crypto 2.0
We learned from crypto that the world is hooked on fads harder than ever. Did crypto itself fail? Not really, Circle just filed a multibillion-dollar IPO, proof that a practical slice of the tech endures even after 99 percent of the hype looks almost pathetic in hindsight. LLMs will follow the same curve. Blockchains stuck around. Why wouldn’t LLMs? Automatic smart switches are awesome.

## A Sci-Fi Future
Read sci-fi from fifty years ago, most of it missed the details. That never mattered, sci-fi is philosophy, morality, and wild ideas, not a product roadmap. Most writers in 1975 could not predict pocket rectangles lit up with addictive content, shops, and ads, complicated by legal drugs of excess, and prescription drugs of austerity. Neural nets do not “change the game,” they add another layer, another brick in the wall. DARPA-net existed before the internet, touchscreens before the iPhone, forums before Facebook.
## Stop Waiting, Start Shipping
Every tool we call “revolutionary” eventually fades into the background of normal engineering. Steam engines, transistors, touchscreens, blockchains, now LLMs, each one looked world‑shaking, then quietly joined the toolbox. The winners were never the loudest cheerleaders. The universe favors makers, not spectators. So pick a project, steal the good parts of the tech, ignore the noise, and press deploy.

Ask yourself, what am I putting off while I wait for a revolution that is already over, or maybe was never coming at all?
