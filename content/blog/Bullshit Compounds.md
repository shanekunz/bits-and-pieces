We assume bad ideas collapse under their own weight. That truth wins eventually.

Sometimes it does. But the deciding factor isn't how bad the idea is, it's whether there's a feedback loop forcing contact with reality.

Markets have feedback loops. Science has peer review. Democracies have elections. These create friction against nonsense.

Remove the feedback loop though, and bullshit doesn't just survive. It compounds. Each generation teaches the next. The workaround becomes the process. The lie becomes "just how things are." A few cycles later, questioning it feels like questioning gravity.

I notice this everywhere now. Legacy codebases. Dev tooling everyone hates but no one replaces. Government systems running on procedures no one remembers the reason for. Teams where "that's just how we do it" is the only answer anyone has.

The mechanism is always the same: cut off outside perspective, give it time, and the dysfunction becomes load-bearing.

The fix is boring. Keep feedback loops open. Protect the people asking "why do we do this?" Be skeptical of any environment that treats outside perspective like a threat.

That's usually a sign something's being protected that shouldn't be.
